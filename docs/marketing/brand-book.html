00

Executive Summary

The hardest part of a brand — a name that means something — is already sitting there unused. This book proposes spending it.

Zagros Dataware runs three distinct practices under one name: enterprise process discipline (BPM, BABOK, ERP), startup validation speed (Lean Startup, Design Sprints, Design Thinking), and process-minded web design for local business. The current identity doesn't say so. The logo is a single decorative peak buried under generic circuit-board ornamentation — the kind that appears on thousands of unrelated "IT consultancy" logos and communicates nothing specific to this business. The tagline names two of the three practices and is silent on the third. There's no short verbal line visitors actually remember and repeat.

The fix isn't a rebrand from zero — it's a tightening. Zagros is a real mountain range: a continuous system of separate, named peaks. That is a more accurate description of this business than anything currently on the website. This book proposes a simplified three-peak mark that encodes the three-practice structure instead of decorating it, a verbal system built around a line already living quietly in the site's own copy — evidence over guesswork — and a channel-by-channel marketing plan that puts the content library already built to work, rather than starting over.

Positioning

Positioning statement

For founders, enterprise operators, and local business owners making decisions on opinion instead of evidence — Zagros Dataware is the process and design consultancy that applies the right discipline at the right speed: BPM and BABOK rigor where structure protects you, Lean Startup and Design Sprint speed where validation saves you, process-minded web design where a clearer path to "buy" is the whole game.

Why "Zagros"

The Zagros range runs over 1,500km across Iran and the South Caucasus — dozens of named peaks, one continuous system. Not a mountain: a range. That distinction is the brand's real asset. Recommend telling this story once, briefly, on the About page — it does more positioning work than another paragraph of service description.

Brand personality

Rigorous. Plainspoken. Unshowy. Evidence-led. Sage/Expert archetype, with a trace of the Architect — structure is the product, not just the process to get there.

Voice & Tone

Not invented for this book — this is already how the site's blog and resource library are written. Codifying it here so it stays consistent as more people write for the brand.

Do — name the mistake plainly The mistake we see most often isn't picking the wrong methodology — it's picking one and refusing to borrow from the other.
Don't — reach for consulting fog We empower organizations to unlock synergistic transformation journeys.
Do — give a real diagnostic question What would happen if this took six more months to finish properly? If the answer involves a regulator or an audit, map it properly first.
Don't — hide behind vague superlatives Our best-in-class methodology delivers unparalleled results for forward-thinking organizations.
Sentence length

Vary it on purpose. A short sentence after two long ones lands like a verdict.

Certainty

Say "typically" and "in engagements of this shape" for patterns; never dress a pattern up as a guarantee.

Banned words

Synergy, leverage (as a verb), holistic, cutting-edge, world-class, seamless, unlock, empower, journey.

04

Logo System

The current mark keeps a mountain silhouette layered under circuit-board ornamentation and a baked-in raster tagline — it doesn't survive a 32px favicon, and the fine detail reads as generic stock-icon rather than specific to this business. The rest of this brand is asking the mark to do one job: be legibly three peaks, so the practice structure is in the logo, not just explained next to it.

ZAGROS DATAWARE
Clear space & minimum size
≥ X ≥ X

X = the height of the middle peak. Keep at least one X of clear space on every side. Never render the icon mark below 20px — below that, use the wordmark alone.

Usage rule

The mono mark (navy or reversed white) is the default, everywhere — header, favicon, business card, contracts. The tri-color mark is a special expression for moments that are specifically about all three practices at once — the about page, this book's cover, a pitch deck's closing slide. Using it everywhere would dilute it into decoration.

Misuse
✕

Don't mirror or rotate the mark.

✕

Don't recolor the mono mark to a single non-brand color.

✕

Don't place on a gradient or busy background.

✕

Don't add drop shadows, bevels, or outlines.

Contrast notes

Range on Paper and white text on Range both pass AA comfortably at any size — use freely for body copy and headers. 07

Imagery Style

No stock photography, no fabricated people or offices. The site already draws its own flat, geometric illustrations for process flows and site mockups — this book codifies that as a deliberate rule, not a placeholder.

Process diagrams — nodes, dashed flow lines, one accent color per state.

Browser mockups — abstract site previews standing in for real screenshots when none exist.

The mark itself — reused as a section divider or background motif at low opacity, never as clip-art scenery.

Content engine

Keep publishing on the existing blog/resource cadence. Every long-form post gets a repurposed LinkedIn version; every illustrative use case follows the same template (starting point → mapping → redesign → typical result) so the format itself becomes recognizable.

Near-term gaps

No lead-capture beyond a mailto link; no testimonials yet from delivered projects; no downloadable/gated version of the technical docs (a natural email-capture lead magnet once there's a list to build).
